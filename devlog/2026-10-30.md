## Why

Project redesign: the system is moving from a single image classifier to a
two-layer architecture:

1. **Image layer** — EfficientNetV2-S classifier on Kaggle dataset loki4514
   "Rice Leaf Diseases Detection" (9 classes, not yet rebuilt)
2. **Weather layer** — EPIRICE epidemic model driven by NASA POWER daily
   weather, producing daily intensity for 5 diseases over a 120-day season
3. **Fusion** — weather risk becomes a class prior; image posteriors are
   reweighted via prior-shift (Saerens et al. 2002)

## Key design choices

### 1. intensity-to-prior formula (`epirice.py::risk_prior`)

```
neutral classes  →  keep train_prior value (unchanged)
active_budget    =  1 - Σ train_prior[neutral]

healthy_frac     =  max(min_healthy, 1 - Σ intensities)   # ∈ [0,1]
epirice share_i  ∝  intensity_i + eps

prior[healthy]   =  healthy_frac × active_budget
prior[disease_i] =  share_i × (1 - healthy_frac) × active_budget
```

**Parameters to ablate in thesis:**
- `min_healthy` (default 0.2) — floor prevents degenerate prior when all
  diseases are high; could be learned from held-out locations
- `eps` (default 0.01) — keeps zero-intensity diseases alive in the prior

### 2. Disk cache keyed by MD5(lat, lon, establishment_date)

R is slow; caching skips the NASA POWER fetch on repeat calls.  Cache lives
in `.cache/epirice/` by default (gitignored recommended).

### 3. `weather_risk/` name

Named `weather_risk/` not `weather/` to avoid confusion with the sky-image
classifier in `train/train_weather.py` / `inference/predict_weather.py`.

## Addendum — lam blending fix (same day)

### Problem

At the start of the growing season (days 1–10) all EPIRICE intensities are
near zero.  The original `risk_prior` (equivalent to `lam=1`) computed a pure
weather prior where every EPIRICE disease class received essentially 0 prior
probability (only the `eps`-floor kept them alive in proportion, but
`epirice_frac ≈ 0` made their absolute share negligible).

Consequence: `prior_shift` with `new_prior ≈ [healthy=1, disease≈0]` would
divide image-model disease probabilities by a near-zero ratio, collapsing them
to zero regardless of how confident the image evidence was.  A leaf-blast field
photo taken on day 5 of the season would be forced to predict "healthy".

### Fix

Added a `lam` parameter (default 0.5) to `risk_prior`:

```
result = lam * weather_prior + (1 - lam) * train_prior
```

`lam=0.5` guarantees every class retains at least half its training-prior
share, so `prior_shift` always has a non-zero denominator and image-model
confidence can still dominate.

### New formula in `epirice.py::risk_prior`

```
weather_prior  ←  (existing intensity → prior formula)
output         =  lam × weather_prior + (1 - lam) × train_prior
```

### Also fixed in cli.py

- Added `--lam` argument (default 0.5).
- Corrected the comment about the training prior source: it must come from the
  **loki4514 rice disease training split**, not `splits/crop_train.csv` (which
  is the old crop-type classifier and is irrelevant to this registry).

### What the plots show

**Intensity curves:**
- All curves start at 0 on their respective establishment date and rise
  monotonically before declining; the shape is sigmoid-like for most diseases.
- `leaf_blast` saturates at 1.0 for the Jul-01 and Jul-15 starts, reaching
  peak on Sep 26 and Oct 13 respectively; the Aug-01 start curve peaks at only
  0.19 on Oct 07.
- `bacterial_blight` and `sheath_blight` show the clearest separation between
  establishment dates: earlier planting → higher peak (0.55 vs 0.41 vs 0.17
  for bacterial blight).
- `brown_spot` is nearly invisible across all three runs (max ≤ 0.003).
- `tungro` rises slowly to a plateau and stays there through the end of the
  season window; it does not show the decline seen in other diseases.
- The x-axes for the three runs do not share the same range (the Aug-01 run
  extends to Nov 29), which is expected.

**Weather (Jul-01 window):**
- Rainfall is concentrated in bursts: notable events around Jul 12 (43 mm),
  Jul 21 (52 mm), Aug 01 (78 mm), Aug 06 (55/44 mm), Sep 24 (21 mm),
  Sep 25 (104 mm — the largest single-day event), Sep 26 (75 mm).
- After the Sep 25–26 rainfall cluster, daily rainfall falls to near-zero
  and does not recover through end of October.
- Relative humidity stays largely between 80–95 % through early September,
  dips briefly in mid-September (~80 %), spikes back around the Sep 25–26
  rain event, then declines to 65–80 % range from mid-October onward.

**Peak timing vs weather:**
- The disease peaks (Oct 05–08 for bacterial blight, sheath blight, tungro;
  Oct 07–13 for leaf blast in the Jul-01 run) occur approximately 10–14 days
  after the Sep 25–26 heavy-rain cluster and coincide with the period when
  daily rainfall has stopped but humidity has not yet dropped substantially.
- By mid-October humidity falls to 65–75 % and rainfall is near-zero; the
  disease curves all show declines or plateaus at this point.
- This pattern is consistent with what the EPIRICE model would compute: the
  model uses temperature, humidity, and rainfall to drive infection rates, so
  a sustained humid period after peak rainfall would extend the disease
  trajectory before the drier post-monsoon conditions suppress it.
  To verify: whether the lag (~10–14 days) matches the incubation periods
  used in the EPIRICE parameterisation for each disease.
